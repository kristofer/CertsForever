What your graduates post

When a graduate shares their certificate, the post carries a full-size image made for it: their name, the course and your school, with a link anyone can check.

  • The link opens the certificate's own page, with the credential ID and the date it was issued.
  • "Add to profile" puts it under Licenses & Certifications on LinkedIn, filled in for them.
  • Every page has a link to your program, so the people looking at your graduates can find you.

How it works

  1. You upload who finished

    A spreadsheet of names, emails and course. Every row is checked and shown to you before anything is issued, and uploading the same file twice is safe.

  2. Each graduate gets a private link

    By email, with one reminder if they haven't looked. A certificate stays private until its owner chooses to make it public.

  3. They add it to LinkedIn

    One click fills in Licenses & Certifications with the credential ID and link, or shares a post with the certificate as its preview.

  4. Anyone can check it

    Each certificate has a permanent page that says who earned what, and when. If you ever revoke one, its page says that too.

For your program

Your look
Your logo, colors, wording and up to two signatures. Certificates keep the design they were issued with, even if you change it later.
Your domain, if you want it
Certificates can live at an address like certs.yourschool.org. Links already shared keep working if you ever change it.
Visitors for your program
Every certificate page links to your site, tagged so the visits show up in your analytics. See views, LinkedIn additions and site visits for each course.
Mistakes you can fix
Correct a misspelled name without breaking the link on anyone's profile. Send a new link to someone who lost theirs.
Your whole team, on the record
Invite colleagues to help. Every change is written to a log you can read, so you always know who did what.
Works with your systems
Export every certificate as a spreadsheet, or let your learning platform issue certificates itself through a private access key.
